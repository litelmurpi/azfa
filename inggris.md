Today, our group is excited to explore one of the most dynamic leadership roles in the tech industry: The Product Manager (PM).

Have you ever wondered why apps like Spotify, Netflix, or Instagram constantly drop feature updates that feel almost mind-reading? Behind every intuitive digital product is a Product Manager working behind the scenes.

As you can see on Slide 1, a Product Manager operates right at the sweet spot where Business ROI, User Experience (UX), and Technology intersect.

A common question is: How is a PM different from a Project Manager? Think of it this way: A Project Manager focuses on 'How & When'—schedules and delivery. But a Product Manager owns the 'What & Why'—identifying real user pain points and driving strategic product vision.""

**[Visual Setup]**

**[Script]**
> "Thanks, Member 1! Now, let's dive into what a Product Manager actually does on a day-to-day basis.
>
> A PM's calendar is dynamic. On any given day, a PM might analyze user feedback, map out product roadmaps, write feature spec requirements, and run sprint planning with software engineers and UI/UX designers.
>
> To excel as a Product Manager coming from an Information Systems background, you need a powerful combination of hard and soft competencies:
>
> * **Hard Skills:** You must master Agile and Scrum frameworks, understand data analytics, create interactive wireframes in tools like Figma, and manage task backlogs in Jira or Trello.
> * **Soft Skills:** The single most crucial skill is **'Leadership Without Authority.'** Since engineers and designers don't report directly to a PM, you must inspire and align cross-functional teams through empathy, strategic clarity, and persuasive communication!"

> Despite these challenges, the career path is incredibly rewarding. Fresh graduates can start as an **Associate Product Manager (APM)**, move up to **Product Manager**, progress to **Senior PM**, and eventually reach executive roles like **Head of Product** or **Chief Product Officer (CPO)**.
